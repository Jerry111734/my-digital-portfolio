Introducing Digital Practices

A reflection on my first week of learning about digital media.

What I learned

This week introduced Digital Practices as more than the use of devices or apps. I considered how technology and society influence each other: people create technologies for particular purposes, but using them can also change everyday habits and relationships.

The social constructionist perspective helped me ask who designs a technology, whose needs it serves and how its meaning can change in different social settings. For example, the same social platform might be used to stay in touch with friends, promote a product or gather opinions for research. These uses depend on people and context, not simply on the software itself.

What this means for my work

An important starting point for my portfolio is to look beyond the technical side of digital media. Learning to build a website matters, but so does deciding who the website is for and how its structure shapes a visitor’s experience. This is a connection I want to keep making as the module moves into web development and data work.

My reflection

I initially found it easier to describe technology by what it does than to explain why it is used in a particular way. The concept of social construction gives me a more useful set of questions: who has a voice in the design, whose experiences are visible and whose needs may be overlooked?

This is a conceptual reflection rather than a report of a practical workshop task. The emphasis this week was on ideas from the lecture and readings.

Next question

As I develop my digital portfolio, how can I make design decisions that suit real readers rather than assuming everyone uses websites in the same way?
