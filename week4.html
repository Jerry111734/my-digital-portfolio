Weekly reflection · WEEK 4

Data Practices and Data Collection

Exploring how students use generative AI in arts and humanities education.

Our research idea

Our group explored a question about whether work remains original when students use generative AI. We focused on students in arts and humanities, where brainstorming, drafting and creative choices may be especially relevant to how people understand authorship.

At the planning stage, we considered public discussions on TikTok, Xiaohongshu and Reddit. I was particularly involved in thinking about the research idea and how to outline it. We needed to decide which kinds of posts would actually help us answer the question, rather than simply collecting any post that mentioned AI.

Choices that shape the dataset

A post about using AI to generate an entire essay is not the same as one about using AI to brainstorm an outline or check grammar. If these examples were grouped into one broad category, the dataset could hide important differences in how students use the technology.

Each platform also has its own audience and style of discussion. Comparing posts across platforms without considering those differences could make the findings misleading. Public online comments would not provide a representative sample of all arts and humanities students.

Ethics and limitations

We discussed the need to treat public posts carefully. A post may contain personal experiences or identifiable information even if anyone can view it. For an academic project, we would need to minimise identifiable details, respect platform rules and avoid quoting people in a way that makes them easy to trace.

This entry describes our research planning and data collection considerations. It does not present a completed dataset or claim that the planned posts were all collected.

My reflection

The main lesson for me was that data collection begins before opening a tool. The research question, platform choice and definition of “AI use” all affect whose experiences become visible. The next step would be to agree on consistent categories and a responsible collection method before analysing posts.
